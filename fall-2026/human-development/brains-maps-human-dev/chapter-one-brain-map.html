Theories of Development

Multidirectional - We don't just grow in one area, and we don't just stay at a standstill either. Humans can grow in multiple areas, in one area, or actually decay or have loss as well.

Lifelong - You don't reach 28 years old and stay stuck like that forever. You grow in some ways, lose other things (coughmalepatterbaldnesscoughcough), and that means you can become a totally different individual at 86 or something.

Multidimensional: We can change across three different domains or dimensions (not literal); physical, cognitive, and psychosocial.

Characterized by Plasticity - How we can accept change basically

Multidisciplinary - Double Dipping in the party dip but in a Human Development lens. He basically recognizes that people can take multiple perspectives into account.

Normative history-graded influences: The period of time in which you were born can have a great deal of importance to your individual characteristics (ex. Baby Boomers -> Talk on Phone, Gen Z -> Life is Phone)

Normative age-graded influences:
An age grade means stages like toddler or teenager

Non-normative life influences:

Multicontextual

Psychosocial - How we interact with those around us.

Cognitive - Changes in our minds, like learning a new language, or taking a Human Development class :)

All three of these Domains interact with each other and change one another. Failure in one can cause others to faulter, and the inverse is also applicable.

Physical - Biological development (puberty, major illness).

Socioeconomic status is also another context, but Paul Baltes didn't feel like adding it in his list I guess. Socioeconomic just means your place in society and tax brackets (businessman vs ceo) also has an effect on your development.
